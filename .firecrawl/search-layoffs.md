  The tech layoff wave is still kicking in 2025. Last year saw [more than 150,000 job cuts](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/31/a-comprehensive-archive-of-2024-tech-layoffs/) across 549 companies, according to independent layoffs tracker [Layoffs.fyi](https://layoffs.fyi/). So far this year, more than 22,000 workers have been the victim of reductions across the tech industry, with a staggering 16,084 cuts taking place in February alone.
  
  We’re tracking layoffs in the tech industry in 2025 so you can see the trajectory of the cutbacks and understand the impact on innovation across all types of companies. As businesses continue to embrace AI and automation, this tracker serves as a reminder of the human impact of layoffs — and what could be at stake with increased innovation.
  
  Below you’ll find a comprehensive list of all the known tech layoffs that have occurred in 2025, which will be updated regularly. If you have a tip on a layoff, contact us [here](https://techcrunch.com/pages/contact-us/). If you prefer to remain anonymous, you can contact us [here](https://techcrunch.com/got-a-tip/).

  ## December
  
  ### Zebra Technologies
  
  Is winding down its autonomous mobile robot (AMR) business, which was built after it acquired Fetch Robotics in 2021, per [a report](https://www.therobotreport.com/zebra-technologies-winding-down-fetch-based-mobile-robot-group/). The Illinois-based company is now considering whether to sell the AMR unit or shut it down, with most employees expected to leave by the end of 2025.
  
  ### Amazon
  
  Is cutting 84 jobs in Seattle and Bellevue in its latest round of layoffs, according to several [media reports](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/filing-amazon-cuts-84-jobs-in-washington-state-unrelated-to-broader-layoffs/). The reductions affect roles across engineering, recruiting, software development, and product management. The layoffs are scheduled for February 2 through February 23, 2026, and affected employees will receive at least 90 days of pay, benefits, and job transition assistance.
  
  ### **Lusha**
  
  Is laying off 8% of its workforce, about 24 employees, as part of a restructuring to reallocate resources toward new growth areas, not broad cost-cutting, [per a report from CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sybqqlwm11g#google_vignette). The Israeli sales intelligence startup will continue hiring for key roles while focusing its product on future market needs.

  ### Tenstorrent

The Santa Clara-based AI chip startup is shifting its focus from enterprise customers to individual developers, while continuing work on its chiplet-based roadmap and broader software and model support.
  
  ### Payoneer
  
  Will let go of [about 30 employees](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/s1fu4c4zzg) in Israel and a similar number of staff overseas, bringing the total reduction to roughly 6% of its global workforce.
  
  ### VSCO
  
  Laid off 24 employees as part of a restructuring to refocus on tools for professional photographers. In an internal memo seen [by TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/09/vsco-lays-off-24-staff-as-its-consumer-business-suffers/), CEO Eric Wittman said that consumer demand fell short and recent expansion efforts hadn’t delivered as hoped.
  
  ### Mobileye
  
  Is [reportedly](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hjznnzvzzl) cutting 200 employees, about 4% of its global workforce. With over 3,000 of its 4,300 employees based in Israel, most of the cuts will affect its local teams.
  
  ### Inside Inbound Health
  
  Shut down on December 1, [according to an audio recording obtained by Axios Pro](https://www.axios.com/pro/health-tech-deals/2025/12/01/inbound-health-closing-home-care-waiver). The hospital-at-home startup had raised more than $50 million.
  
  ## November
  
  ### Intel
  
  The company continued with its stated goal of [cutting a significant amount of its workforce this year](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/22/intel-reportedly-plans-to-lay-off-over-21000-employees/), with 59 Bay Area jobs eliminated effective November 30, [in a Employment Development Department filing caught by KRON4](https://www.kron4.com/news/technology-ai/intel-lays-off-bay-area/).
  
  ### HP
  
  Is [reportedly](https://www.reuters.com/business/hp-cut-about-6000-jobs-by-2028-ramps-up-ai-efforts-2025-11-25/) set to cut 4,000 to 6,000 jobs worldwide by 2028 as it looks to streamline operations and leverage AI to speed up product development and boost efficiency.
  
  ### Apple
  
  Is cutting several sales positions handling accounts ranging from business and schools to government agencies, as it moves to streamline how it sells devices and services to businesses, schools, and government agencies, [Bloomberg reports](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-24/apple-aapl-cuts-jobs-across-its-sales-organization-in-rare-layoff).
  
  ### Monarch Tractor
  
  Told employees it may lay off more than 100 workers or even shut down, according to an internal memo [obtained by TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/19/monarch-tractor-preps-for-layoffs-and-warns-employees-it-may-shut-down/). This comes after weeks of staff cuts across the autonomous electric tractor startup’s California offices and its teams in India and Singapore.
  
  ### Playtika
  
  Announced plans to lay off about 20% of its workforce, 700 to 800 employees, next month, marking its fifth round of cuts since 2022, [according to Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/h1y4ejyg11g). The Nasdaq-listed gaming company, valued at $1.5 billion, employs about 3,500 people.
  
  ### Pipe
  
  Has laid off about 200 employees, roughly half its workforce, per [Fintech Business Weekly](https://fintechbusinessweekly.substack.com/p/celtic-bank-faces-another-lawsuit). The revenue-based small business lender, once valued [at $2 billion](https://techcrunch.com/2021/05/19/pipe-raises-250m-at-a-2b-valuation/), said the cuts are part of its push toward profitability and greater operational efficiency.
  
  ### Synopsys
  
  Plans to cut roughly 10% of its workforce and close several sites as part of a restructuring tied to its recent acquisition of Ansys, [The Wall Street Journal reported](https://www.wsj.com/business/synopsys-to-cut-10-of-workforce-close-sites-in-restructuring-b4d735f3). The layoffs, which are expected to affect about 2,000 employees, are scheduled to take place during fiscal 2026, which began November 1.
  
  ### Deepwatch
  
  Has laid off between 60 and 80 employees, citing artificial intelligence as one of the factors behind the decision, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/12/cybersecurity-firm-deepwatch-lays-off-dozens-citing-move-to-accelerate-ai-investment/). The cybersecurity firm, which builds an AI-powered threat detection and response platform, employs roughly 250 people.
  
  ### Axonius
  
  Is [reportedly](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sj0syry1wg#google_vignette) cutting roughly 10% of its staff, notifying employees in early November that about 100 of its 900 workers will be laid off. The New York–based cybersecurity firm says the move aims to streamline operations.
  
  ### MyBambu
  
  Is set to permanently close its local operations, laying off all 141 employees in two waves, [according to a filing with the Florida Department of Commerce.](https://cbs12.com/news/local/new-west-palm-beach-fintech-firm-to-lay-off-141-employees-amid-funding-collapse-layoff-south-florida-palm-beach-county-downtown-west-palm-beach-news-mybambu-a-fintech-startup-2751-s-dixie-hwy-420-november-5-2025) The Florida-headquartered fintech company’s first 100 employees were let go on October 31, with the remaining 41 slated for termination by December 31.
  
  ### Hewlett-Packard
  
  Is removing 52 positions at its San Jose campus, according to reporting from [the San Francisco Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/tech/article/layoffs-hpe-hitachi-vantara-21140900.php). The layoffs, which began last month and will continue through November, affect employees across cloud development, engineering, and product management.
  
  ## October
  
  ### Amazon
  
  [After Reuters reported that the company was planning to eliminate](https://www.reuters.com/business/world-at-work/amazon-targets-many-30000-corporate-job-cuts-sources-say-2025-10-27/) up to [30,000 corporate jobs](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/amazon-reportedly-plans-to-cut-around-30000-corporate-jobs/), amounting to roughly 10% of its 350,000 employees in their corporate departments, [Amazon shared that it would pursue](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-workforce-reduction) an “overall reduction in our corporate workforce of approximately 14,000 roles.” Since that news broke, [Amazon has laid off 660 employees across multiple New York City offices](https://www.usatoday.com/story/money/2025/11/11/amazon-nyc-layoffs-restructuring-november-2025/87221740007/), with more to come through the year.
  
  ### Rivian
  
  Is [cutting 600 jobs](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/23/rivian-reportedly-cutting-600-workers-in-third-layoff-of-the-year/), about 4% of its workforce, amid an EV market pullback, marking its third layoff this year. Details of the latest layoffs remain undisclosed, while earlier cuts in June and September affected 100 to 150 employees in its commercial and manufacturing teams.
  
  ### Meta
  
  [Has laid off approximately 600 employees](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/22/meta-cuts-600-ai-jobs-amid-ongoing-reorganization/) across its AI infrastructure units, including the Fundamental AI Research (FAIR) team and other product-related roles. However, top-tier AI hires in TBD Labs, managed by new chief AI officer Alexandr Wang, will not be affected.
  
  ### Applied Materials
  
  [Plans to cut about 4% of its workforce, or roughly 1,400 jobs](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/applied-materials-to-lay-off-4-of-workforce-to-simplify-operations-4306282), to streamline operations amid tighter U.S. semiconductor export controls.
  
  ### Handshake
  
  [Laid off around 100 employees in October](https://joinhandshake.com/blog/our-team/our-refounding-in-the-ai-era/), about 15% of its 650-person U.S. workforce. The layoffs affected various roles across its recruiting business vertical. The San Francisco-based startup is an online platform connecting college students and recent graduates with employers for early-career jobs.
  
  ### Smartsheet
  
  Has [reportedly](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/smartsheet-cuts-jobs-shortly-after-departure-of-longtime-ceo/) laid off over 120 employees amid a leadership transition following CEO Mark Mader’s retirement. The enterprise software company, which grew to more than 3,300 employees, was acquired for $8.4 billion by Blackstone and Vista Equity Partners earlier this year, taking it private.
  
  ### Google
  
  Has cut over 100 design roles in its cloud division, hitting U.S.-based teams especially hard, as the company shifts focus toward AI investments, per [a CNBC report](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/01/google-cloud-unit-layoffs.html). Many affected employees have until early December to find a new role within Google, following additional layoffs across its Silicon Valley offices, including at least 50 permanent cuts in Sunnyvale.
  
  ### Paycom
  
  [Is reportedly laying off over 500 employees](https://www.oklahoman.com/story/business/information-technology/2025/10/01/paycom-layoffs-2025-workers-replaced-with-ai/86448337007/) due to AI and automation improving back-office efficiencies. The Oklahoma City-based HR and payroll software company will provide affected workers with severance packages, outplacement services, and access to internal job opportunities.
  
  ## September
  
  ### Just Eat
  
  Will [eliminate around 450 jobs](https://www.reuters.com/business/world-at-work/just-eat-lay-off-around-450-employees-partly-automating-operations-2025-09-25/) as part of a cost and operations review, according to Reuters. The layoffs will span multiple functions and countries, including customer service and sales. Europe’s largest food delivery company said it is increasingly using automation and AI, shifting many manual service tasks to automated systems.
  
  ### **Fiverr**
  
  [Plans to cut around 250 jobs](https://www.wsj.com/business/fiverr-to-cut-about-250-jobs-in-refocusing-effort-reiterates-guidance-8f15ee4b), approximately 30% of its workforce, as part of a push to become a leaner, faster, and AI-focused company, according to The Wall Street Journal. The Tel Aviv-headquartered freelance services marketplace said the restructuring will reduce management layers and position it to pursue growth with an AI-native approach.
  
  ### ZipRecruiter
  
  Is closing [its Tel Aviv development center, cutting about 80 jobs](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/pwjukv20s). Led by Yosi Taguri, the office specialized in software, data, and AI research, including algorithm development. The California-based recruitment firm, founded in 2010, is trimming costs amid a challenging labor market.
  
  ### GupShup
  
  Has [laid off at least 100 employees](https://inc42.com/buzz/exclusive-gupshup-lays-off-100-employees-in-cost-cutting-exercise/), including junior developers, just months after cutting nearly 200 jobs. The San Francisco-based conversational AI company, which is preparing for an IPO within two years, [raised $60 million in equity and debt in July](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/22/gupshup-raises-60m-in-equity-and-debt-leaves-unicorn-status-hanging/).
  
  ### xAI
  
  Laid off about a third of its data annotation team, [cutting roughly 500 jobs](https://www.businessinsider.com/elon-musk-xai-layoffs-data-annotators-2025-9), according to Business Insider. The move comes as the company shifts focus from generalist AI tutors to specialist roles, after testing workers to assess their strengths. Employees were told they’ll be paid through the end of their contracts — or November 30 at the latest — but their system access was cut immediately, Business Insider reports.
  
  ### Rivian
  
  Has [reportedly](https://www.wsj.com/business/autos/rivian-layoffs-ev-manufacturer-1f20bc7f) laid off about 200 workers, or 1.5% of its staff, as the company braces for the end of federal EV tax credits under President Trump’s policy changes. The $7,500 incentive for new electric cars expires this month, adding to pressure from cooling demand. Despite the cuts, Rivian says it’s moving ahead with plans for a lower-cost model.
  
  ### Oracle
  
  Is cutting another [101 jobs in Seattle](https://www.seattletimes.com/business/cloud-computing-giant-oracle-lays-off-more-seattle-workers/) and 254 [in San Francisco](https://www.kron4.com/news/technology-ai/oracle-bay-area-layoffs/), just weeks after a wave of layoffs in August. The company, which had about 3,900 local employees before the cuts, hasn’t explained the move and declined to comment.
  
  ### Salesforce
  
  Is trimming another [262 jobs at its San Francisco headquarters](https://www.kron4.com/news/bay-area/salesforce-laying-off-262-employees-in-san-francisco-after-ceo-praises-ai/), according to a state filing, with layoffs set to take effect November 3. The move comes just weeks after CEO Marc Benioff touted AI’s potential to cut customer support roles and follows [a smaller round of cuts in Seattle and Bellevue](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/salesforce-lays-off-93-employees-in-washington-state-as-benioff-touts-ai-driven-efficiencies/) earlier this month.
  
  ## August
  
  ### Cisco
  
  Will [eliminate 221 positions](https://www.sfchronicle.com/tech/article/cisco-oracle-layoffs-bay-area-20824135.php) across its Milpitas and San Francisco offices, including 157 in Santa Clara County and 64 in San Francisco, effective October 13, according to filings with California’s Employment Development Department reported by the San Francisco Chronicle. The cuts are part of the company’s broader workforce-reduction strategy.
  
  ### Restaurant365
  
  [Laid off about 100 employees last month](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/tech-supplier-restaurant365-lays-9-staff), around 9% of its workforce, after falling short of ambitious growth targets. The cuts affected staff across all departments. The company provides back-office software for restaurant chains.
  
  ### Oracle
  
  Is [set to cut 101 jobs at its Santa Clara location](https://www.kron4.com/news/technology-ai/cisco-and-oracle-to-cut-hundreds-of-bay-area-jobs/), with notices issued on August 13 and terminations effective October 13. The company, which recently disclosed [nearly 200 layoffs](https://www.kron4.com/news/technology-ai/oracle-lay-offs-bay-area/) at its Pleasanton and Redwood City offices, is also planning to [lay off 161 employees in Seattle](https://esd.wa.gov/employer-requirements/layoffs-and-employee-notifications/worker-adjustment-and-retraining-notification-warn-layoff-and-closure-database), according to filings with the Washington state Employment Security Department.
  
  ### F5
  
  Is [cutting 106 positions](https://esd.wa.gov/employer-requirements/layoffs-and-employee-notifications/worker-adjustment-and-retraining-notification-warn-layoff-and-closure-database) at its Seattle and Liberty Lake, Washington, offices, according to a state Employment Security Department filing. The layoffs, which affected senior engineers and managers, are part of a broader global workforce reduction, although the security and application delivery company has not disclosed the total number of employees affected.
  
  ### Peloton
  
  [Will cut 6% of its workforce in its sixth layoff](https://www.theverge.com/news/754154/peloton-q4-2025-earnings-layoffs-wellness-price) in just over a year. Peloton CEO Peter Stern said the cuts are needed to improve long-term business health.
  
  ### Kaltura
  
  Is cutting [10% of its workforce](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/6a8duhohw), or about 70 employees, as part of a cost-saving effort to reduce operating expenses by $8.5 million, marking its third round of layoffs since 2022. The corporate video software company plans to maintain and gradually grow its sales and marketing budgets, driven by a robust pipeline and growing adoption of its AI-powered offerings.

  # Character.AI enters the microdrama arena with its own productions, but there’s a twist
  
  [Ivan Mehta](https://techcrunch.com/author/ivan-mehta/)
  
  6:00 AM PDT · July 9, 2026
  
  Microdramas are [such a rage](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/23/tiktok-like-microdramas-are-going-to-make-billions-this-year-even-though-they-kind-of-suck/) these days that nearly every kind of company in the attention economy space — be they dedicated [microdrama apps](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/21/indias-app-downloads-rebounded-to-25-5-billion-in-2025-fueled-by-ai-assistants-and-microdrama-boom/), social media giants (TikTok and Instagram) or streaming services ( [Peacock](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/11/bravo-is-creating-unscripted-microdramas-for-the-peacock-app/), [Amazon Prime](https://deadline.com/2026/03/amazon-mx-player-fatafat-microdrama-service-india-1236762793/), and [India’s JioHotstar](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/tadka-jiohotstar-100-million-users-microdrama-1236771461/)) — is building a product to tap the opportunity.
  
  Character.AI, which lets people chat with customized AI avatars, is also tapping this budding market by producing its own microdramas using AI characters. But there’s an interesting twist that takes advantage of the company’s core product: Users older than 18 can chat with these shows’ characters, ask them questions, and even roleplay different storylines.
  
  The startup is launching three microdramas to start with: a romance series dubbed “Last Summer,” a horror show titled, “The Nighttime Game,” and a Hunger Games-like survival microdrama called “Eden Fall.”
  
  Character.AI says these dramas were created using AI production tools, and in the long term, it aims to help users create their own characters and series.
  
  “Starting with a studio-led model, c.ai Series lets our production team develop the format, refine the workflow, and understand what audiences want from Character-native Microdrama entertainment. Over time, the goal is to turn those learnings and workflows into creator tools, enabling users to make their own series from original Characters and share them with a global audience,” a company spokesperson told TechCrunch.
  
  This is the latest in a slew of recent features from the startup following its shift toward [entertainment-focused](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/25/character-ai-will-offer-interactive-stories-to-kids-instead-of-open-ended-chat/) features last year. In April, it teased a tool called [Lorebook that users can employ to create world-building information that characters](https://blog.character.ai/pipsqueak2-and-more/) can reference, and launched [another feature](https://variety.com/2026/gaming/news/character-ai-launches-ai-powered-books-feature-1236722570/) called Books that lets users insert themselves into select classic literature titles, or role-play as characters from them.
  
  The company said on Thursday that it is also testing a feature, dubbed c.ai FM, that will let users put together audio series, and another that lets you create fiction, called c.ai Reads. The audio series feature is currently available to select users under its experimental c.ai Labs program, which the company says professional writers are using to create serialized audio dramas.
  
  There’s certainly an audience for this form of entertainment. Users spent more than 950 minutes on Character.AI each month in the first half of 2026, according to [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/report/state-of-ai-2026/download).

  Let me tell you something that would have saved hundreds of developers months of frustration: the job market you knew in 2022 doesn’t exist anymore. Not temporarily down, not in a cyclical dip — fundamentally restructured. And if you’re still applying to jobs the old way, you’re fighting a battle that’s already lost.
  
  Over 700,000 tech workers lost their jobs between 2022 and 2025. That’s not a typo. Seven hundred thousand. To put that in perspective, that’s roughly the entire population of Seattle getting laid off. But here’s what the headlines miss: companies are still hiring — just not the same way, not in the same places, and definitely not for the same roles they used to fill.
  
  I spent weeks digging through layoff trackers, job market data, compensation reports, and dozens of first-hand accounts from developers who lived through this. What I found wasn’t doom and gloom — it was something more interesting. A complete reshaping of where developer talent is valued, what skills companies are betting on, and which industries are quietly competing for engineers while everyone’s still staring at FAANG job boards.

  Here’s the timeline that explains why your inbox went quiet:

  **2023:** 264,320 layoffs. The worst year since the dot-com crash. This was the year Meta cut 21,000 people, Amazon cut 27,000, Google cut 12,000, and Microsoft cut 10,000 — all in the span of a few months. Stanford Professor Jeffrey Pfeffer documented what he called “copycat layoffs” — companies imitating each other’s terminations regardless of actual business need. It was social contagion at the executive level.

  # List of Companies Announcing AI-Driven Layoffs
  
  Last Updated: July 9, 2026
  
  A growing number of companies are citing artificial intelligence and automation as reasons for cutting staff.
  
  In many cases, executives said AI systems are replacing key roles and allowing teams to operate with fewer people.
  
  We reviewed hundreds of company disclosures, earnings calls, and major media reporting to identify layoffs where AI or automation was directly named as a driver.
  
  ## More than 165,000 employees have been affected by AI-related layoffs in 2026
  
  More people than ever are at risk of losing their jobs due to advancements in AI.
  
  ![](https://programs.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/ChatGPT-Image-Jun-22-2026-01_49_34-PM-1024x512.png)
  
  Just halfway through the year, that’s already an increase of approximately 50% over 2025’s total figure.
  
  ## The number of AI-related layoffs has increased by more than 8x since 2024
  
  The exact number of global AI layoffs is hard to quantify. Nonetheless, based on announcements from high-profile organizations, the figure has skyrocketed in a very short space of time.
  
  ![](https://programs.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/ChatGPT-Image-Jun-23-2026-02_43_06-PM-1024x683.png)
  
  In 2024, layoffs and freezes from a group of seven international companies led to approximately 18,000 AI-related job losses.
  
  That number increased by at least 5x in 2025, resulting in over 155,000 layoffs due to AI.
  
  In the first 6 months of 2026, more than 155,000 roles have already been cut.
  
  ## In total, 55+ CEOs have announced layoffs due to efficiencies made by AI
  
  AI-based layoffs are becoming increasingly widespread.
  
  ![](https://programs.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/ChatGPT-Image-Jun-22-2026-01_53_48-PM-1024x576.png)
  
  Since 2025, at least 9 companies have announced AI-related layoffs affecting 10,000+ employees each.
  
  These include:
  
  - Accenture
  - Amazon
  - Citigroup
  - Dell
  - HSBC
  - Intel
  - Microsoft
  - TCS
  - UPS
  
  ## More than 60% of announced layoffs occurred at companies with over 100,000 employees
  
  ![](https://programs.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/ChatGPT-Image-Jun-22-2026-01_59_37-PM-1024x576.png)
  
  And AI layoff announcements aren’t restricted to tech companies either. They’re coming from industries like finance, logistics, consulting, media, retail, and manufacturing.
  
  ## Major Recent AI-Related Layoffs and Hiring Freezes
  
  Below is a company-by-company breakdown showing who cut or froze roles, how many employees were affected, and how AI factored into each decision.
  
  ### Accenture
  
  **No. of Employees Impacted:** ~11,000
  
  **Industry:** IT Consulting
  
  **Date:** December 2025
  
  Accenture said it would cut roughly 11,000 roles as part of a restructuring tied to how work is changing inside the firm. [Accenture CEO Julie Sweet made clear that “those we cannot reskill will be exited”](https://www.cxtoday.com/contact-center/accenture-lays-off-11000-staff-as-part-of-ai-reskilling-strategy), instead focusing on automation and AI tools for internal tasks (particularly in non-client-facing functions). The company emphasized it would continue investing to retrain certain employees whose roles could evolve.
  
  ### Amazon
  
  **No. of Employees Impacted:** ~30,000
  
  **Industry:** Technology / E-commerce
  
  **Date:** October 2025, January 2026
  
  In late 2025, Amazon eliminated about 14,000 corporate jobs as it flattened management layers and pushed for faster decision-making. [Beth Galetti, Amazon’s Senior Vice President, pointed to advances in AI as one reason the company could operate more efficiently with fewer people](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/amazon-confirms-14000-corporate-job-cuts-says-push-for-efficiency-gains-will-continue-into-2026), noting that automation was increasingly embedded across internal processes. [This was followed by a further 16,000 at the start of 2026](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/amazon-cuts-16000-jobs-globally-broader-restructuring-2026-01-28/).
  
  ### ASML
  
  **No. of Employees Impacted:** ~1,700
  
  **Industry:** Technology
  
  **Date:** January 2026
  
  The world’s leading supplier for the semiconductor industry announced recently initial cuts of 1,700 jobs, making up 3.8% of the total staff. This comes at a time when the Dutch tech company is riding the AI boom, [announcing record orders as a result](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/1/28/tech-giant-asml-announces-record-orders-in-boost-for-ai-boom). Despite this, the company plans to cut a further 1,300 jobs, citing efficiency as a primary reason, as ASML chief financial officer Roger Dassen explains: [“As our FY 2025 financial results demonstrate, we are choosing to make these changes at a moment of strength.”](https://fortune.com/2026/02/10/ai-washing-and-forever-layoffs-why-companies-keep-cutting-jobs-even-amid-rising-profits/)
  
  ### Atlassian
  
  **No. of Employees Impacted:** 1,600
  
  **Industry:** Technology
  
  **Date:** March 2026
  
  Software tool developer Atlassian announced a 10% reduction in its global workforce due to changes needed for [the “AI era”](https://www.businessinsider.com/atlassian-layoff-global-workforce-attributes-it-to-the-ai-era-2026-3). Notably, Atlassian founder and CEO Mike Cannon-Brookes highlighted that team members with “transferable skills” were spared this round of layoffs.

  **Date:** January 2026

Nonetheless, this comes at a time when [AI tools are fueling Autodesk’s share gains](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/jpm-upgrades-autodesk-as-cloud-ai-tools-drive-share-gains-4479277).

  **Date:** February 2026

The cuts primarily affect support staff rather than lawyers, including hundreds of roles across research, marketing, know-how, and secretarial functions. A spokesperson said [the decision followed a review of business operations aimed at improving efficiency through AI and refocusing investment on roles that best serve clients](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/law-firm-sacks-hundreds-ai).

  ## What tech layoffs happened in 2025?
  
  The total number of tech layoffs in 2025 was 122,549 across 257 companies according to layoffs.fyi., which tracks job cuts in the tech industry. That’s down from 152,922 employees laid off from 551 companies in 2024, according to layoffs.fyi.
  
  Companies that announced layoffs in waves of more than 10,000 workers included some of the biggest names in tech: Intel, Amazon, Tesla, Google, Meta and Microsoft.
  
  ## When did all of the tech layoffs start?
  
  Roger Lee, creator of Layoffs.fyi, has been following layoffs in tech since 2020 as startups started laying off employees during the early days of the pandemic. According to Lee, the pandemic created an opportunity for people to increasingly turn to the Internet for work, shopping and socializing. In response, tech companies went on a hiring spree to meet consumer demand.
  
  This growth in tech employment started in late 2020 and lasted through 2021. At the same time the Federal Reserve’s policy slashed interest rates throughout 2021, which enabled tech companies to raise capital and invest in growth, Lee said. But both trends reversed in early 2022.
  
  The majority of layoffs at the beginning of 2022 came from startups, according to Lee. But in late 2022 and early 2023 it started to creep into bigger tech, as well. Lee also said that “Big Tech” layoffs like those seen at Meta and Twitter “present a unique opportunity to recruit a caliber of talent that would've previously been impossible to attract.”
